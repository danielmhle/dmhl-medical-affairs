Medical Insights Collection and Triage for Field Medical Teams

A controlled-document format demonstration for field medical operations.

1. Purpose

To define a consistent, compliant process for capturing, documenting, assessing, routing, and closing the loop on unsolicited scientific insights received by field medical colleagues.

2. Scope

Applies to Medical Science Liaisons (MSLs), field medical leaders, and designated Medical Affairs operations personnel who document or manage insights in the approved CRM or insight management process. It covers routine scientific insights and does not replace adverse event, product complaint, or urgent medical information reporting procedures.

3. Responsibilities

  • MSL: capture the insight faithfully, document context without interpretation, and escalate time-sensitive information through the appropriate process.
  • Field Medical Lead: coach for quality and consistency; review trends and address overdue routing or follow-up.
  • Medical Affairs Operations: maintain the taxonomy, workflow configuration, and periodic quality review.
  • Insight owner / receiving function: evaluate routed items, document disposition, and return an appropriate response or status.

4. Definitions

Insight
A de-identified, actionable observation or question arising from scientific exchange that may inform medical strategy, evidence generation, education, or related decisions.
Triage
Initial assessment of relevance, urgency, quality, and appropriate recipient.
Source context
Non-identifying information about the setting and type of interaction needed to interpret the observation.

5. Procedure

Capture at the point of exchange

  1. Listen for a scientific question, unmet need, evidence gap, treatment-pattern observation, or other potentially actionable theme.
  2. Do not prompt for confidential patient details. If patient information is volunteered, follow privacy policy and do not reproduce identifying details in the insight record.
  3. When a potential adverse event or product complaint is mentioned, follow the applicable reporting procedure immediately; do not rely on the insights workflow.

Document in the approved system

  1. Create an insight record linked to the appropriate account and interaction, using the approved taxonomy and a concise, factual title.
  2. Record the source context, date, scientific theme, and a clear summary in the HCP’s own meaning. Separate direct observation from MSL interpretation.
  3. Do not include promotional language, unsupported conclusions, or patient-identifiable information. Use the designated field for any clearly labeled field interpretation.

Assess quality and priority

  1. Check that the record is understandable, sufficiently contextualized, and not a duplicate of an existing theme.
  2. Apply the approved priority criteria: potential impact on patient care or evidence strategy, urgency, recurrence, and confidence in the source context.
  3. Flag urgent or potentially safety-related matters using the relevant escalation process and document only the permitted cross-reference.

Route and track disposition

  1. Select the receiving Medical Affairs team or other authorized function according to the routing matrix. Commercial routing, if permitted by policy, must remain limited to appropriate non-promotional business insights and must not disclose protected scientific exchange content.
  2. Submit within the local target timeframe (illustrative target: two business days). The system owner monitors queues and overdue records.
  3. The receiving owner records a disposition, next step, and due date where follow-up is needed.

Close the loop and review

  1. Where appropriate, the MSL shares a balanced scientific response with the HCP through a suitable channel, without implying that the insight guarantees a particular action.
  2. At regular intervals, Medical Affairs reviews trends, recurring evidence gaps, routing timeliness, and data quality. Aggregate reporting should use minimum necessary information.
  3. Retain, correct, or archive records according to the organization’s records management schedule.

6. References

  1. Applicable company policies for adverse event and product complaint reporting.
  2. Company privacy, records retention, and information security policies.
  3. Approved CRM user guide and medical insights taxonomy.
  4. Applicable industry codes and local requirements governing scientific exchange.

Reference titles are representative. Confirm current controlled versions and local applicability before use.
